# Lab w3l2: the judge prompt and the one call

> *"Educational software. Not investment advice. No orders are placed; trades are hypothetical and settled from historical bars by written rules. Past results, including these, do not predict future results."*

Part of [week 3 labs](index.md). Previous: [lab w3l1](w3l1.md).

**What you build:** the request side of `p1_judge` in `src/tradingresearch/evals.py`: the rubric in the judge's instructions, the thesis as the text being graded, the cited claims and the post-trade summary as its evidence (never the bars), and one call, bounded in tokens and capped in dollars. Part B (lab w3l3) keeps the run in a run file, reads the reply with your `p1_verdict`, stores the verdict and returns the Score; until then `p1_judge` sends its request and stops with `NotImplementedError` naming the run. **Start:** `tr lab start w3l2` after lab w3l1 (or `tr lab start w3l2 --assume-previous` to start on the reference solutions of w3l1). **Cost:** $0. The provider is the repo's real OpenRouter adapter with its HTTP transport replaced: every request gets the one recorded reply, nothing leaves your machine, and the key is the string `not-a-real-key`.

> **The repo's own suite does not move in this lab.** `tests/test_week3.py` stays at 6 of 19 passing: its judge tests call `p1_judge` to the end and check the returned Score, the stored verdict and the run file, which part B writes. These checks look at the request instead, and they stay green through part B, which does not change the request. `tr lab next` runs the repo suite without the judge tests (the `deselect` list in [`lab.toml`](../lab.toml)).

**Reading folded in (not exercises):** `evals/rubrics/p1_judge_prompt.txt` (the frame: what INPUT and OUTPUT hold, the `{rubric}` line), `label.cited` and `label.summary` (the evidence lines `tr label` shows the operator, reused so the judge and the operator see the same evidence), ADR-003 (the judge model, `JUDGE_MAX_TOKENS`, the ~500 output tokens per trade estimate) and gigai-core's `llm_judge`, `run_eval` and `CallBudget`. They are in the chapter, [§4 The P1 judge and `tr grade --judge`](https://scout.edgelab.one/learn/ai-engineering/week-3/#4-the-p1-judge-and-tr-grade---judge-2-h-0). [Concepts](concepts.md) has the short version.

**The data.** One closed trade and one recorded exchange. The trade is `tests/test_week3.py`'s synthetic FAKEA thesis (`closed()`): long, filled at 101 on 2026-11-17, closed at its target of 106 the next session, graded; the same trade the chapter's acceptance tests judge. The exchange is the request and the `openai/gpt-6-sol` reply the repo's tests replay, run `run_54953ec7ce484649`, copied from `tests/fixtures/week3/` into [`data/judge_request.json`](../data/judge_request.json) and [`data/judge_reply.json`](../data/judge_reply.json), with the model's price row in [`data/models.json`](../data/models.json). [`tools/fakea.py`](../tools/fakea.py) builds the trade in a temporary journal and runs your `p1_judge` on it.

**Why the checks compare against the recorded request.** A replayed reply answers whatever you send: the recording says pass to an empty prompt too. It is evidence about your code only for the request that produced it. So every check in this lab compares your request with the recorded one, a part at a time: the instructions, OUTPUT, INPUT, the bound and the cap, and last the whole body.

**What observe can and cannot show.** `observe.py judge` runs your `p1_judge` once on the FAKEA trade and prints what it sent against the recording: the model, `max_tokens`, the prompt's length, how many of the rubric's 7 sections it holds, INPUT and OUTPUT line counts and whether they equal the recording's, how many raw bar values leaked in, whether the whole body equals the recording, and what happens on a second run under a cap of $0.001. It also prints the verdict the recorded reply reads as, which is always pass: that line never moves, and that is the point. Observe shows one trade; the checks decide.

### w3l2.3 one bounded call

**Concept.** One call per trade, bounded twice. In tokens: `CallSettings` defaults to 1024 output tokens; ADR-003 sets `JUDGE_MAX_TOKENS = 4000` ("estimates about 500 output tokens per trade; this bounds a runaway reply"), and a reply cut at the bound can end before its verdict (w3l1.3's `cut_off`). In dollars: a `CallBudget(max_usd=..., prices=...)` estimates the call's worst case before sending it and raises `BudgetExceeded` when it does not fit: nothing sent, nothing spent. The estimate is the prompt counted at about 4 characters a token plus all 4000 output tokens, at the price table's rates: $0.042964 here. It is an estimate: the recorded call's prompt was really 1558 tokens, not 1482, so its true worst case was $0.043116. The cap is the `max_usd` the caller passes (`tr grade --judge` will pass its own), not a number fixed in `p1_judge`. [Call budget](concepts.md#call-budget).

**You edit** `p1_judge`, from the settings to the end ([stub](../exercises/w3l2.3-one-bounded-call/stub/src/tradingresearch/evals.py)):
