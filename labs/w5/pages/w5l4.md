**Reading folded in (not exercises):** why a time-series test period must come after the training data, and why the sessions right before the cutoff are dropped (a training example whose outcome is still open at the cutoff reads test-period prices); why 20, the longest hold, was two sessions short; what the id covers (the chapter: the digest covers the cutoff, the embargo and the Prereg; why `registered_at` stays out of it is this lab's own argument, in w5l4.2); the open question that a later split can still be registered and quietly govern. ### w5l4.1 the embargo floor

**Concept.** The [embargo floor](concepts.md#embargo-floor): a swing thesis written after session S enters within the next 2 sessions and exits at most 20 sessions after its entry, so its outcome reads bars up to S + 22. With an embargo of 22 sessions, the last train/val session's latest exit is the session before the cutoff. A `TrainingSplit` with a shorter embargo is refused, and the refusal says why. The floor is a lower bound: every integer under it is refused, zero and negatives included, and a longer embargo is a stricter split, never refused. It follows `HOLD_RANGE`, whichever horizon holds longest: the check builds a copy of `records.py` with a longer swing hold, and one with a longer short hold, and expects `MIN_EMBARGO` and `EMBARGO_WHY` to move with it. The refusal's text is checked word for word: `tests/test_split.py`, which `tr lab next` runs at the end of this lab, and `tr split register`'s output match it exactly, so a message that says the same thing in other words is a wrong fix.

### w5l4.3 a cutoff still ahead

**Concept.** [Registered before the cutoff](concepts.md#registered-before-the-cutoff): a split is only a held-out period if it is written down before that period starts. `register` refuses, with `split.Refused` and before it writes a row: an embargo under the floor (before the lookup, so a stored split cannot slip past it), a cutoff that is not an NYSE session, one not after the last completed session, and one whose session opens no later than now or than the pre-registration's own `registered_at`. A cutoff that breaks several rules gets the first in that order. The calendar decides in every year, 2028 as much as 2026 (the check pins the NYSE calendar to 2020–2030 so the day you run it does not matter), and a `registered_at` exactly at the open is not before it. Each refusal's text is checked word for word: `tests/test_split.py`, run at the end of this lab, and `tr split register`'s output match it exactly, so a message that says the same thing in other words is a wrong fix.

### w5l4.4 the derived sets

**Concept.** The [embargo window](concepts.md#embargo-window) is the `embargo_sessions` NYSE sessions right before the cutoff session, oldest first: holidays are not sessions, so 22 sessions before 2026-12-29 reach back to 2026-11-25, and 40 before 2026-12-01 reach back to 2026-10-05. The [derived sets](concepts.md#derived-sets) are computed when read, never stored: each distinct journal session goes in one set, train/val before the window, embargoed from the window's first session up to the cutoff, test on or after the cutoff, with no end (a 2028 session is test). `partition` leaves the list it was given as it was, and `derive(conn, split_id)`, already written, is `partition` over the sessions the journal's `analysis` rows hold: the check inserts rows and reads the sets through it.
