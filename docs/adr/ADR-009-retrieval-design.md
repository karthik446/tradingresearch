# ADR-009: retrieval design: which retriever feeds passages to analyze-3

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-17 (week 6). The retriever choice below rests on the recall eval, which cost nothing.
Whether passages help an analysis was judged by one complete run of `experiments/retrieval.toml` against the rule
pre-registered below: the retrieval default stays `none` (see Result).

**Context.** Week 6 lets a fixed-context arm add the top k chunks of a filing index to the analyze-3 prompt
(`[arms.retrieval]`, `masking.augment`). What the index holds, who may see it and the injection risk are in
`docs/retrieval-scoping.md`. Before spending on an experiment, the retriever has to find the right text: four are
built (`evaluate.RETRIEVERS`), and this ADR picks one with a labelled query set.

The evidence: index `sha256:71917448df40d8bedb619d7937e674591cb3124fcf38a3a12b66758136e761eb` (10 tickers, 166
documents, 8,951 chunks) and the 30 queries of `evals/retrieval_query.jsonl`: 10 `lookup` and 8 `event` questions
written by hand from press-release and 8-K text, and 12 `numeric` questions generated by
`evals/make_numeric_queries.py` from the 10-Q line items in SEC companyfacts. Each query names a ticker and a
session; retrieval sees only that ticker's documents public by the session close (240 future documents masked over
the 30 queries). recall_any@k is the share of queries with at least one gold chunk in the top k; recall_frac@k is
the mean share of a query's gold quotes found. Run:
`TR_DATA_DIR=$PWD/data-dev uv run tr retrieval eval --retriever bm25 dense hybrid hybrid+rerank` (9.06 s, CPU).

**Options considered.**
- `bm25`: keyword scoring over the chunk text. No model, nothing to download.
- `dense`: cosine similarity of vectors from BAAI's bge-small-en-v1.5, run as the ONNX export
  `Qdrant/bge-small-en-v1.5-onnx-Q` at revision `aa8f8b060edb`.
- `hybrid`: reciprocal rank fusion of the bm25 and dense lists.
- `hybrid+rerank`: the hybrid top 20 reordered by the cross-encoder `Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`.
- No retrieval: analyze-3 as in week 5, SOURCES only. This is the `none` arm of the experiment.

**Reading the tables.**
- hybrid+rerank is highest at every k on all 30, hybrid ties it at k = 20 (0.77). bm25 and hybrid tie at k = 5
  (0.60); dense is lowest overall.
- The gap is small and n is 30. There is no paired bootstrap for recall in the repo, so here are plain counts of
  queries one retriever finds in its top 5 and the other does not: hybrid+rerank vs hybrid, 2 won, 0 lost, 28 tied;
  hybrid+rerank vs bm25, 4 won, 2 lost, 24 tied; hybrid vs bm25, 2 won, 2 lost, 26 tied. Two to six queries
  decide each comparison, so none of these orderings would surprise anyone if it flipped on another 30 queries.
- Per kind: bm25 finds all 8 event quotes in its top 5, where named entities and exact phrases ("Alegeus",
  "Item 7.01") favour keywords. The reranker's gain is in lookup questions (any@5 0.90 vs 0.70 for the other three).
- Numeric questions are the weak spot everywhere: any@5 is 0.25 to 0.33, and 6 of the 12 are missed at k = 20 by
  every retriever. Their gold is a table row such as `Net income | $15,305`, which shares few words with "What net
  income did ... report for the quarter ended ...", and a 10-Q repeats a label across tables (quarter and year to
  date, segments). Three of the six numeric concepts (revenue, net income, diluted EPS) already reach analyze-3 as
  SOURCES rows from companyfacts (`analyze.FUNDAMENTALS`), so passages matter most for the other three and for
  prose.
- One query, q12 (CAT's third-quarter 8-K, "Item 7.01. Regulation FD Disclosure."), is a date test: every CAT
  earnings 8-K has that line, and only bm25 ranks the right filing in its top 5.

**Decision.**
- The experiment arm uses `hybrid` with k = 5 against the pinned index above (`experiments/retrieval.toml`). At
  k = 5 hybrid matches bm25 on all 30 (0.60) and beats it on numeric (0.33 vs 0.25), and it does not lean on one
  scoring method when the query wording drifts from the filing's.
- `hybrid+rerank` is not chosen for now. Its lead over hybrid at k = 5 is 2 queries out of 30, and it adds a second
  model to download and pin. If the experiment shows passages help, a second run with `mode = "hybrid+rerank"` is
  the next comparison, with this table as the prior.
- `dense` alone is dropped: lowest recall_any at k = 1, 5 and 20.
- The default query stays `store.DEFAULT_QUERY` ("{ticker} quarterly results: revenue, earnings, guidance, outlook
  and risks"). On the 20 experiment ticker-days it returns 5 passages each: 72 from press releases, 24 from 10-Qs,
  4 from 8-K cover documents and no headlines.
- The experiment's decision rule is the pre-registration below, written before any paid call. By it the retrieval
  default stays `none` (Result).

**Pre-registration (written before the run, 2026-11-17; not to change after it).** One run of
`experiments/retrieval.toml`: arm `none` (analyze-3, SOURCES only) against arm `hybrid` (the same prompt plus the top
5 hybrid passages from the pinned index above), `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`, the 20 ticker-days of weeks 4 and 5.
Exactly one paid run; no rerun for a better result. If `tr exp run` refuses a comparison, the same gigai-core
`compare` runs on the stored runs (`tr exp compare`, $0), one grader at a time, and the ADR says so.
- **Unit:** the ticker-day, paired by item (hybrid minus none), clustered by ticker (10 clusters), 95% interval from
  `tr exp` (gigai-core `compare`).
- **Primary:** `claims_valid`, hybrid minus none. Hybrid is **non-inferior** if the lower bound of the 95% interval
  is above −0.05. The margin is a rule chosen before the run, not a measured number: a loss of up to 5 points of
  valid claims per analysis is accepted as the price of passages that are used and cited correctly.
- **Secondary (reported, descriptive, not deciding):** `all_claims_valid`; `parsed`; the share of hybrid items in
  which any claim cites a passage (items with a `citation_accuracy` score over all 20 hybrid items, failed calls
  included in the 20); `citation_accuracy` in the hybrid arm, the mean over the items that have graded claims, with
  that n stated and no interval claimed if n is below 10 items; mean input tokens, cost and latency per item for
  each arm.
- **Decision:** the retrieval default stays `none` UNLESS all three hold: (1) hybrid is non-inferior on
  `claims_valid`; (2) passage-citing claims occur in at least 25% of hybrid items (5 of 20); (3) the hybrid arm's
  `citation_accuracy` is at least 0.90. Then the default becomes `hybrid`, k = 5, on this index.
- **Otherwise:** no change, and the result is a measured negative, read as such. It covers one model, one prompt
  and 20 ticker-days (10 tickers, two adjacent sessions with the same retrieved passages per ticker). A model that
  cites no passage says nothing about retrieval quality, only that this prompt did not lead it to use passages. A
  failed condition is not evidence that passages hurt, unless the interval says so.

**Amendment 1 (2026-11-17, before the rerun; the thresholds above do not change).** The first run of this registration
is **void for infrastructure, not for its result**. The `none` arm finished (`run_e9d0ebaf14da424e`, 20 items, $0.3306).
The `hybrid` arm (`run_f965ff876c724f65`) completed 4 of 20 items ($0.1012); the other 16 were refused by OpenRouter
with HTTP 402 `in_flight_budget_exhausted` before any output, because the account balance was too low. Nothing was
decided from it. The people running the experiment did look at the 4 completed hybrid items and at the `none` arm, so
the rerun is not blind to them; the decision rule is the one registered above and is not retuned. The registration's
"exactly one paid run" is read as "one complete run": this rerun is that run. Changes, and only these:
- **Provider and route.** Both arms run on one provider, Anthropic's API directly (`TR_PROVIDER=anthropic`, route
  `anthropic:claude-sonnet-5-5`), not OpenRouter. The config still names `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` (the same
  model); the route differs, so the void run's `none` arm is not reused and neither arm is compared across providers.
- **Cap.** `max_usd_per_arm = 1.00` per arm is unchanged. Cost uses the Anthropic price table of the adapter
  (ADR-014).
- **Sampling noise.** The `none` arm sent requests byte-identical to week 5's `full` arm on all 20 items and still
  scored differently (claims_valid 0.9350 against 0.9636), so identical input does not give identical scores. The
  interval from `tr exp` covers item-to-item variation within one pair of runs; run-to-run noise on identical input

- A second incomplete run is not retried: if the rerun is cut short, the result is reported as incomplete.

`tr exp` paired the 20 items in 10 ticker clusters (hybrid minus none, 95% interval): `claims_valid` −0.038
[−0.081, +0.008] (Wilcoxon p = 0.264, MDE 0.071); `all_claims_valid` −0.250 [−0.500, +0.050] (McNemar p = 0.18);
`parsed` +0.000 [+0.000, +0.000]. It skipped `citation_accuracy` (not scored in the none arm, which has no passages).
The three passage-citing items are UNH 11-12, KO 11-12 and AAPL 11-13, one passage-citing claim each, three distinct
chunks.

**Result decision, by the registered rule.**
- (1) Non-inferiority on `claims_valid`: **fails.** The lower bound, −0.081, is below −0.05.
- (2) Passage-citing claims in at least 25% of hybrid items: **fails.** 3 of 20 items (15%) against 5 needed.
- (3) `citation_accuracy` at least 0.90: holds on its face, 1.000, but over n = 3 items, so no interval is claimed
  and it rests on three claims.
- **The retrieval default stays `none`.** Two of the three conditions fail, so nothing changes; this is a measured
  negative, read as registered.

**Reading the result (what it shows and what it does not).**
- It shows that with this prompt and model, five passages in the request rarely become cited evidence: 3 of 222
  hybrid claims cite a passage. It does not say the retriever found the wrong text; a passage the model does not cite
  is not graded, so retrieval quality is not tested here beyond the recall table above.
- It does not show that passages hurt. The `claims_valid` interval runs from −0.081 to +0.008 and contains 0; the
  rule failed because the interval also reaches below the −0.05 margin, not because a loss was measured. Losses
  smaller than about 0.071 (the run's MDE) are likely to go undetected at n = 20.
- Sampling noise: the same analyze-3 prompts with no passages scored `claims_valid` 0.964 in week 5's `full` arm
  (`run_c32ea07781a54982`, OpenRouter), 0.935 in the void run's `none` arm (`run_e9d0ebaf14da424e`, OpenRouter) and
  0.971 here (Anthropic; input tokens per item equal to week 5's on all 20 items; 4 items scored differently), a
  spread of 0.036 across two routes. Identical input does not give identical scores. The interval from `tr exp`
  covers item-to-item variation within one pair of runs; run-to-run noise on identical input is separate and is not
  covered: week 5's `full` against the void `none` (byte-identical requests, both OpenRouter) gives `claims_valid`
  −0.029 [−0.053, −0.003] and `all_claims_valid` 0.850 → 0.600 under the same `tr exp compare`. A difference of that
  size between two runs, such as the −0.038 here, is not by itself evidence of a prompt effect. *(Corrected
  2026-11-17 after the run, thresholds and decision rule untouched, as in Amendment 1.)*
- Post hoc, not registered, decides nothing: `all_claims_valid` moved by the same −0.250 (0.850 → 0.600) between
  those two identical-input runs, so its 0.850 → 0.600 here is not to be read as an effect of the passages.
- Post hoc, not registered, decides nothing: 8 hybrid analyses fall below 1.000 on `claims_valid` against 3 in none
  (12 and 17 at 1.000), and 7 of those 8 carry a `number_in_text` rejection (a digit written into the claim text),
  against 2 of 3 in none. Whether those digits came from the passages was not checked.
- One model, one prompt, 20 ticker-days: 10 tickers over two adjacent sessions, which retrieve the same passages per
  ticker, so the 20 items are closer to 10 retrieval cases. analyze-3 says how to cite a passage only in the passage
  banner; the result does not carry to a prompt that asks for passage citations, another model, `hybrid+rerank` or
  another k.

**Consequences.**
- Nothing in the pipeline changes: retrieval stays an experiment option (`[arms.retrieval]`) and no default prompt or
  command gains passages. Passages cost 2,201.3 more input tokens per item here (2,050 to 2,422; 74.3% over none's
  2,963.7) and 45.2% more per analysis ($0.024861 against $0.017127), for 3 cited passages in 20 analyses.
- The `hybrid+rerank` run named in the Decision was conditional on passages helping; this result does not trigger
  it. Any further retrieval experiment needs its own registration and run.
- The recall numbers belong to this index digest and query set. A rebuild (new filings, another chunker or
  embedding revision) gives a new digest, and the eval has to be rerun before any number here is quoted again.
- Numeric recall stays low until a query can name the row the way a filing labels it, or a table row is indexed
  with its column header; both are retriever work, not prompt work.
- A retrieval arm grades with code graders only (`experiment.run_arms` refuses a judge), so the experiment cannot
  say whether a claim's prose is faithful to a passage, only whether its cited chunks were retrieved and hold its
  value (`evals/specs/citation_accuracy.md`).
- Passages are untrusted text in the prompt. The banner and the 5,000-character cut are the only guards in week 6;
  week 7 adds more.
